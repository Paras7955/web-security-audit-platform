# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

ScopeHarbor is primarily for application-security practitioners and security-minded developers auditing local targets and repositories they are authorized to test. They need to move confidently through a security-check workflow, understand what the system is doing, and make remediation decisions without having to decode an expert-only scanner interface.

## Product Purpose

ScopeHarbor provides a defensive, local-first workflow for running authorized application-security audits, reviewing normalized findings, prioritizing risk, and producing clear remediation outputs while sensitive evidence stays under the operator's control. Success means users can complete an audit safely, follow each step without confusion, and understand their findings through a visually appealing presentation that makes risk and next actions easy to grasp.

## Positioning

ScopeHarbor makes authorized application-security audits safe, understandable, and visually clear without surrendering control of sensitive evidence.

## Brand Personality

Focused, visually appealing, and trustworthy. The product should communicate calm competence: serious enough for security work, approachable enough to guide users through it, and polished enough that complex findings feel structured rather than overwhelming.

## Anti-references

ScopeHarbor should not resemble a hacker-themed terminal or an intimidating expert-only scanner. Avoid visual or interaction patterns that obscure the workflow behind security theater, assume unexplained specialist knowledge, or make users feel uncertain about the next step.

## Design Principles

1. **Make scope and safety visible.** Users should always understand what is authorized, what will happen next, and which boundaries the platform enforces.
2. **Guide the audit as a coherent workflow.** Progressive disclosure, clear state changes, and actionable next steps should make complex security work easy to follow.
3. **Turn findings into decisions.** Present risk, evidence, and remediation guidance with strong hierarchy and visual clarity instead of raw scanner noise.
4. **Earn trust through restraint.** Use precise language, familiar controls, and predictable behavior; visual polish should support comprehension rather than imitate hacker aesthetics.
5. **Keep sensitive work under the operator's control.** Reinforce the local-first model and never trade safety or privacy for convenience or spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support full keyboard operation, visible focus states, sufficient text and interface contrast, status communication that does not rely on color alone, and reduced-motion preferences. Security terminology and workflow guidance should remain understandable to users who are not scanner specialists.
